What stoat.chat teaches us

Stoat — formerly Revolt — is the closest thing pqp has to a five-years-ahead version of itself: one developer, AGPL, self-hostable, a Discord lookalike, a million registered users. Their public record is unusually complete, and most of it is a warning.

2026-08-08 · primary sources read from their public GitHub org · see the method note below for what was and wasn't readable

Headline
Every growth spike Stoat has ever had came from a Discord crisis they didn't cause and couldn't absorb — and Brazil is the current crisis, with Stoat not even in the conversation.

They have run out of capacity at every wave (registrations gated, presence and typing indicators switched off, “the current architecture is unsustainable”), had video and screenshare code-complete but switched off for cost during their biggest-ever demand spike, designed a paid tier twice and launched it never, and refused to build a Discord importer on legal grounds — leaving the single largest adoption gap to third-party tools. The lesson for pqp is not a feature list. It is: be ready to absorb a wave, have a payment rail before you need it, and be the obvious Portuguese-language answer while the slot is empty.

Method — what I could and couldn't read

stoat.chat blocks Claude's fetcher. Their robots.txt carries User-agent: ClaudeBot / Disallow: / plus Content-Signal: ai-train=no, and https://stoat.chat/updates returns HTTP 403 to automated requests. I did not spoof a browser User-Agent to get around that. The same block applies to status.stoat.chat, translate.stoat.chat, api.stoat.chat, support.stoat.chat, their internal wiki hosts, and the legacy wiki.revolt.chat.

It turned out not to matter. The blog is an Astro site whose repository is public: every post lives as a committed markdown file in stoatchat/stoat.chat. So every first-party post quoted here is a primary source read from GitHub — a different host, openly published, no circumvention. Everything else comes from the GitHub API (commits, releases, issues, discussions, PR diffs, locale catalogues), UK Companies House, Bluesky's public API, Hacker News, and press.

Genuinely unread: the rendered blog and changelog pages, the status-page incident history, the Weblate dashboard (percentages below are my own arithmetic over their .po and JSON catalogues), their internal roadmap and design-doc hosts, and Reddit (also blocks ClaudeBot). Never published anywhere: MAU/DAU, donation totals, any financials — their first Companies House accounts aren't due until November 2026.

A warning about secondary sources: most of the 2026 “Stoat review” pages that surface in search (stoatboard.com, reviewnexa.com, pnmmedia.com and similar) carry fabricated user counts and claim Stoat has end-to-end encryption. It does not — E2EE has been an open issue since 30 Oct 2021. Even Xataka Brasil's article got this wrong. Nothing from those sources is used here.

03The voice saga — read this one twice

This is the most directly transferable thing in the whole document, because it is the exact decision pqp has already made differently.

2021-08   Vortex begins — custom SFU, Rust, mediasoup
          ...never leaves "(in development)" status...
2024-01   last substantive Vortex commit
2024-04   LiveKit port attempt #1 (PR 318) — abandoned
2024-11-03 Vortex formally deprecated: "rewriting completely to use LiveKit"
2025-05-01 LiveKit port attempt #2 opened (PR #414, "voice chats v2")
2025-11-12 ...merged. +3,349 / -659 across 102 commits
2026-01-10 ships in backend v0.9.0
2026-02-16 publicly confirmed generally available

           elapsed: 4 years, 5 months

Three and a half years building a custom SFU that never shipped, then eighteen more months porting to LiveKit across two attempts. And it didn't end there: they now maintain hard forks of livekit-server and livekit-protocol (created 2025-10-23, with patches through July 2026 adding mute/unmute webhooks, room telemetry and participant counts) — because a chat app needs presence signals an SFU doesn't natively emit.

What this validates, and what it warns about

Validates: pqp's LiveKit decision is the ending of Stoat's story, reached at the start instead of after 4½ years. docs/voice-backends.md, the server-decides-and-pins-the-transport design, and “a client that cannot use it is refused rather than silently split off” are all the mature version of what Stoat arrived at the hard way.

Warns about two things. First: the cloudflare-sfu stub in pqp is Vortex-shaped — a half-built alternate transport that falls back to mesh. Vortex sat in “(in development)” for three years and was never archived even after deprecation. Either finish it, delete it, or label it dead loudly enough that nobody mistakes it for a plan. Second: expect to patch LiveKit. Stoat needed participant counts and mute webhooks badly enough to fork the server. pqp keeps presence on /ws in both modes, which is a better answer to the same problem — but budget for the discovery that the SFU doesn't tell you something you need.

And the part that should genuinely alarm you

Video and screenshare were code-complete but not enabled on the flagship instance, for bandwidth cost. Self-hosters got it around March 2026; the hosted service — the one with a million registered users, during the largest migration wave in its history — did not, because there is no money. The for-web repo shipped the screen-picker and screenshare-audio UI in May and June 2026 for a feature the flagship couldn't afford to turn on.

pqp's mesh voice has exactly this economics. Mesh is free because peers pay; LiveKit and TURN are not. The moment pqp's voice is good enough to be the reason people come, its cost curve becomes the constraint — and Stoat is the proof that “we'll monetise when we need to” fails, because the moment you need to is the moment you have no time.

The tactics that visibly worked

  • Being the drop-in lookalike at the moment of a Discord crisis. Not a strategy so much as a standing readiness — and the thing they were never quite ready for.
  • Counter-positioning on a specific subtraction. Discord removed discriminators; Stoat added them and said so loudly. Cheap, legible, and it converted.
  • GitHub Discussions as the town square. 1,294 discussions, upvote-ranked, maintainers answering in public. It doubles as a roadmap, a support channel and a legitimacy signal — and it costs nothing to run.
  • Bluesky as the outage channel. Posting “there's simply too many people” in real time during a wave reads as authenticity rather than failure.
  • Transparency artefacts. Public crisis logs, incident postmortems, milestone posts with self-deprecating humour, joke locales in Weblate (OwO, Pirate, Bottom, Enchantment Table), a Supporter badge at £3.

And what visibly didn't

  • No announcements pipeline since October 2024. The rebrand — the single biggest event in the project's history — was never announced in their own Discussions.
  • The tagline backlash. “Please don't brand stoat as ‘a chat where you are the main character’” is the second-most-upvoted discussion in the project's history (286 upvotes), posted 2026-02-10 — at the peak of the wave, by the new arrivals.
  • Losing a chunk of each wave to missing table stakes. TechCrunch (2026-03-01) and other coverage listed the same gaps every time: mobile voice, threads, no importer.

05The problems they hit in public

Scaling

Their October 2024 crisis log (discussion #789, 131 upvotes, 95 comments) is worth reading in full: registrations arriving at 2–3 dozen per second, signup gating via BlockedByShield, two DDoS waves, a database migration performed solo across roughly nine-hour days, and an explicit note about “taking longer breaks away to avoid burnout.”

“The current architecture is unsustainable and is hitting bottlenecks… there's not enough bandwidth to go around.”

insertish, for-web issue #557, 2026-02-13

A read-replica fix during that wave silently dropped events and had to be reverted. StatusGator has recorded 48 outages since April 2026, including a multi-hour one on 2026-08-02.

Direct hit on pqp

docs/HANDOVER.md lists “move rate limiting + presence to Redis before running more than one API instance” as suggested-next-work item 8 of 10. Stoat's history says that's the item that decides whether a wave becomes users or becomes a status page. server/src/lib/rate-limit.ts is in-process and its own header explains how N replicas multiply every limit — so today pqp cannot scale horizontally at all, which means a wave has exactly one failure mode. That item belongs near the top, and it belongs there before any marketing.

Moderation and abuse

  • A roughly two-person trust-and-safety function. The admin-panel README offers support only “if you contribute financially or via code, as we are currently very much resource constrained.”
  • Parts of that admin panel are closed-source inside an AGPLv3 repository — an open licence-violation issue against themselves.
  • “We simply don't have the bandwidth to manually handle such a plethora of spam accounts” (DeclanChidlow, 2026-01-18).
  • Their own no-reply address landed on the Spamhaus blocklist in January 2026.
  • No automod. No CSAM hash-matching in the codebase — ClamAV only. A CSAM-policy clarification PR has sat unmerged since June 2025 in a now-archived legal repo. The first “moderation API for pulling reported images” shipped this week, August 2026.
  • Their approach to the UK Online Safety Act is a geoblocking microservice created on 2025-07-25 — the day the OSA's children's duties took effect — that does exactly one thing: flag GB via a Cloudflare header so the client can age-gate. A direct question about UK compliance in their discussions has no maintainer answer.
The comparison that should be uncomfortable

pqp is ahead of a million-user competitor here, and that is not a compliment to either party. pqp has a real 18+ gate with a DOB check, an instance-level report queue, automated reports, member timeouts, an audit log with retention, blocks, and a fail-closed content-scan architecture with 36 provider tests. Stoat, at 1M registered users, has ClamAV and two people.

But pqp's CONTENT_SCAN_PROVIDER is empty, so the scanning is architecture without a provider — and HANDOVER item 4 notes the OSA illegal-content risk assessment was due 16 March 2025 and the NCA CSEA-IRP registration went live 7 April 2026 with no size threshold. Stoat's answer to the same obligation was a geoblock and silence. Don't benchmark against that. Being better than the incumbent alternative is not the same as being compliant, and three of the four items on that list cost nothing.

Funding — the sharpest lesson in the document

Donations only: Ko-fi, Boosty, a £3 Supporter badge. “Financial transparency reports coming soon™” has been promised since May 2024 and never delivered. And then this pattern:

          integration and a full "Section 8: Subscriptions"
2025-10-20 A Mollie payments SDK is vendored into the repo
           ...referenced by zero services...
2026-02   Migration wave. Discussion threads full of "Let us pay you!"
2026-04   Video/screenshare still off on the flagship, for bandwidth cost

A paid tier designed twice and launched never, while the users were actively asking to pay and the product was rationing features for want of money. Maintainers state repeatedly that they volunteer around day jobs — “again, I have a day job” (insertish, 2025-11-18); “I don't have enough free time to fully maintain it anymore” (Zomatree, on iOS, 2026-02-10).

Federation — refused, consistently, since 2021

Worth correcting the record: they never promised federation and then dropped it. They have refused it since September 2021, and the clearest statement of why is from 2026-02-15 (discussion #1073, 212 upvotes): building it would be “a herculean effort… we'd rather put that time into features that appeal to more people (such as screen sharing, DM encryption).” The older FAQ put it more bluntly: “Matrix does federation well and we don't intend to compete with it.”

The recurring debates are largely a documentation artefact: the rebrand broke the FAQ links that answered the question, so the argument gets re-litigated every wave. That's a cheap, avoidable, self-inflicted cost.

Self-hosting

2,572 stars on the deploy repo, and a support burden with little return. Official clients still cannot point at your own instance (open since April 2025, blocked on a backend issue). Self-hosters get no admin panel, no Discover, no automod. The config file's documentation request was closed as not-planned with “the documentation is the root file.” And the honest summary, from the founder:

“I don't believe we ‘advertise’ self hosted support.”

insertish, self-hosted#141, 2025-11-18

They eventually made two community volunteers maintainers of that repo (2026-02-25) — the correct move, arrived at late.

Security advisories, for calibration

Unrestricted account creation (2024-06); a webhook token leak permitting full-history fetch (2025-02-10); and an unauthenticated SSRF in their embed service plus a stored XSS in the web client, both 2026-05-17. pqp has server/src/lib/safe-fetch.ts with SSRF guards and body caps, and 36 fail-closed scan tests — the shape of defence Stoat added after the advisory.

Trust incidents

  • AI-code disclosure. Three trivial Claude-authored commits triggered a disclosure demand; all three were reverted and the thread was locked (2026-01-08; it also made Hacker News). pqp is an agent-built codebase. This is worth a position before it's a controversy.
  • Branding backlash as the second-most-upvoted discussion ever (§04).
  • Separately and to their credit: Makles publicly called out Andrew Tate's “The Real World” for AGPL-violating use of Revolt's code (2022-12-05).

06The importer they refused to build

This connects directly to Doc 1, and it independently confirms the line that doc draws.

“We won't be implementing message scraping from discord… Enabling others to do this on their own instances could still bring us under fire if discord wishes to do so.”

IAmTomahawkx, 2025-05-04, discussion #905; restated 2026-02-22

Roughly fourteen import requests since 2021. The refusal is specifically about message scraping — and they explicitly left open the possibility of a data-export-based self-import, which nobody has built. Meanwhile the gap is filled by third-party tools (rambros3d's Discord Migrator, LazyCat2/discord-to-revolt) that do structure only, no history.

Convergent conclusion, arrived at independently

Doc 1 recommends a structure-only importer via Discord Guild Templates and recommends against message history. Stoat — with a million users, four years of requests, and every incentive to build it — reached the same split from the legal side: history is the part they won't touch, and structure is the part their community built for them.

Two things follow. One: the structure-only importer is an unclaimed wedge in this market, not a commodity — the leading open alternative has publicly declined to build it. Two: Doc 1's Guild Template route is better than what the third-party cloners do, because it needs no bot in the user's server and no privileged intents, which is precisely the exposure Stoat's maintainers were worried about.

08Mobile and app-store reality

The brief asked specifically about app-store pain. The honest finding: no documented Apple or Google rejection or removal, anywhere. Their mobile problems are entirely self-inflicted, and that's more useful.

PlatformStateLesson
Android Native Kotlin/Compose. Early Access on Google Play since 2025-02-07 — four years after launch. Google Play only, with Google's PairIP DRM applied. Unavailable in Russia and Belarus (open issue, unanswered — nobody knows if that's Google or a developer setting). The self-run F-Droid repo has been orphaned since 2022 and still serves a three-year-old build. An orphaned distribution channel is worse than none. A three-year-old build sitting on a live F-Droid repo actively damages trust.
iOS Native Swift. TestFlight only, marked “not yet ready for production,” at tester capacity since ~June 2026, and its maintainer said in February 2026 he no longer has time. No evidence it was ever submitted for App Store review. Last push 2026-06-14. The bottleneck was never Apple. It was one person's spare time. A native iOS client is a permanent staffing commitment, not a project.
Desktop Electron. The 1,029-star repo was abandoned at the rebrand and replaced with a fresh one — losing the stars, the issue history, and the search ranking. Rename repos, don't replace them. GitHub redirects renames for free.
For pqp specifically

pqp is currently a PWA plus an Electron shell plus a new native SwiftUI iOS client in ios/ — untracked in git as of this branch. Stoat's iOS story is what that becomes without a sustained owner: a TestFlight build at tester capacity, publicly marked not-production-ready, whose maintainer has run out of hours. docs/PWA.md's reasoning (“no separate mobile codebase — it is the same SPA”) is the cheaper posture, and the native client is a deliberate bet against it. Worth being explicit about which one is the primary mobile story, because Stoat is what “both” looks like after five years.

The finding that matters most in this document

Brazil is a live, regulator-driven Discord crisis with no incumbent alternative holding mind-share, and the leading open-source alternative is half-translated and absent from the coverage.

The context, from Doc 3's legal research: ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025) has been in force since 17 March 2026 with ANPD enforcement extending beyond app stores this month; the STF's June 2025 ruling made platforms liable for user content without a court order. Yesterday's TechTudo piece reports the First Lady calling for Discord to be taken down after the Naviraí case, with an AGU civil action. This is the exact shape of every event that gave Stoat a 24× spike — except it's in Portuguese, and the slot is empty.

Two things pqp needs in order to be in that story: a Portuguese-first landing page on a domain that exists (HANDOVER item 6: pqp.gg is unregistered, and the canonical and OG tags already point at it, so shared links currently render broken previews), and the capacity to survive being in it (HANDOVER item 8). Neither is a feature.

10So what for pqp — ranked

01

Register pqp.gg and ship a Portuguese-first landing page this month, because the Brazilian Discord crisis is happening now and the alternative slot is provably vacant.

Evidence: TechTudo covered a potential Discord ban in Brazil on 2026-08-07 and named TeamSpeak, Teams and Slack — not Stoat, whose new client is 69.7% translated to pt-BR and which has exactly one original Brazilian article, containing a false E2EE claim. pqp side: HANDOVER item 6 — the domain is unregistered while client/index.html canonical/OG tags already point at it.

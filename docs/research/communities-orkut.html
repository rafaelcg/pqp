Communities, from Orkut

Three concepts lifted from the mechanics that made Orkut Brazil’s social network — comunidades, depoimentos, recados — adapted to a real-time chat app rather than cloned, with the moderation and liability bill attached to each.

2026-08-08 · sourced from Brazilian press, SciELO, and Wikipedia pt/en · pqp surfaces read at launch/p0-hardening

Recommendation
Build Depoimentos first. ~4 agent-days, zero new public surface, and it’s the single feature Brazilians of this demographic name unprompted when asked what they miss.

Comunidades is the bigger idea and the right second bet — but it moves pqp out of the “private messaging” bucket that Brazilian platform-liability law currently treats gently, and into the public-content bucket, while CONTENT_SCAN_PROVIDER is still empty. Enquetes (polls) is a cheap, genuinely-missing third. The mural/scraps wall I recommend against outright, and §07 says why with receipts.

01Why Orkut, and why now

Not nostalgia bait. Orkut is the only social product that ever won Brazil outright, and the mechanics it won with are structurally different from everything shipping in 2026 — which makes them cheap differentiation rather than table stakes.

No timeline, no status updates, no algorithmic distribution. Communities spread by being visible on profiles. That constraint is the whole design insight.

Facebook overtook Orkut in Brazil in August 2011 (Ibope Nielsen: 30.9M vs 29M) and comScore confirmed it for December 2011 (36.1M vs 34.4M). Orkut then lost more than 95% of regular users in three years and closed on 30 September 2014. Nobody should reconstruct 2009. But the reason nostalgia for it is still press-friendly eleven years later is that nothing replaced these specific mechanics — Facebook, Instagram and Discord all won on feeds and real-time, and none of them ever shipped a friend-written, owner-approved testimonial or a public identity badge you collect for jokes.

The demographic, stated plainly

Orkut nostalgia lands with Brazilians roughly 30–45. That's not Discord's centre of gravity, and that's the point: it's an audience Discord doesn't serve well, reachable by a mechanic Discord doesn't have. It's also an audience with disposable income and no current home for adult-friendship-group chat. But be honest that eleven years is a long gap and the nostalgia is now largely a media phenomenon — recurring listicles, anniversary coverage, a Threads trend in 2025. It converts attention, not necessarily retention (§06 has the receipts on that).

02What actually made these mechanics sticky

Comunidades were badges, not forums

Free to create, self-service, no approval. You picked a name, a category, a description and a mandatory representative image, chose aberta or moderada, and you were live. One dono, up to about ten moderadores single-source. Inside: a topic forum (tópicos), enquetes, a member list, events, and a related-communities module. Discovery was keyword search plus browsing by category — and, critically, a dedicated section on your profile listing every community you were in.

That profile section is the mechanic. Brazilian retrospectives say it flatly: communities were “uma forma de mostrar do que gostavam ou o que pensavam, já que havia uma área do perfil destinada exclusivamente às comunidades das quais a pessoa era membro.” The academic framing is sharper still. Bezerra & Araújo (2011, SciELO) call the Orkut profile a vitrine pessoal — a shop window — in which “se enfeixam traços (comunidades a que se pertence) para se vestir uma identidade”: you dress an identity by bundling traits, where the traits are your memberships.

The number that proves it

Google's shutdown archive preserved 51 million communities and 120 million topics. That's an average of ~2.35 topics per community across the platform's entire ten-year life. (derivation, not a cited statistic)

The median Orkut community never hosted a conversation. Joining was the entire product. Any design that treats comunidades as “forums we should make active” has misread what people were doing — and will build engagement machinery for a feature whose value is delivered at the moment of the click.

A member count plus a single owner creates a market

Eu odeio acordar cedo was sold on 7 July 2009 by its creator for roughly R$ 3,000–5,000; the buyer used its millions of members to promote an airline-ticket site. Eu AMO Floripa!! was bought by Grupo RBS, handing the buyer a ~75,000-person audience — protest communities sprang up questioning the sale's legality, and the moderators deleted the criticism. There were cartels of large-community owners cross-promoting growth, with a tabela de preços for community sales single-source.

Design consequence, and it's not optional: any public group primitive with a visible member count and a transferable single owner will grow a secondary market in audiences that never consented to the transfer.

Depoimentos were the approval, not the text

Mechanics, from a contemporary May 2011 how-to: open a friend's profile → Depoimentos in the left sidebar → write → Postar. Then “agora é só aguardar que seu amigo aceite seu depoimento.”

The recipient had to accept it, and until they did it was not public. Depoimentos were private by default and publication was the subject's own affirmative act. That is the entire load-bearing difference from every review, endorsement and wall-post mechanic since. Rich formatting was supported — bold, colour, emoticons, embedded YouTube, photos, raw HTML.

Culturally, accepted depoimentos were displayed “como um troféu no perfil,” and “quanto mais depoimentos uma pessoa recebia, mais popular ela era entre os amigos.” Friends competed over o top — who would stay longest as the most recent depoimento on a given profile. In August 2024, at Rio Innovation Week, Büyükkökten himself named depoimentos as the feature people ask for most. Brazilian tech press agrees by revealed preference: it's in the headline of nearly every retrospective.

“Não aceita!” — the design lesson you must not skip

Because unaccepted depoimentos sat in a pending queue readable only by the recipient, indefinitely, Brazilians discovered that a depoimento was a private message. People wrote confessions and romantic declarations opening with the warning “Não aceita!” — don't accept this. And the failure mode — the recipient accepting it anyway, publishing an intimate message to their whole profile — became canonical Brazilian internet folklore.

Two things follow. First: an approval queue that retains rejected items will be repurposed as a private channel, so design for that on purpose. Second: accidental publication of a private message is a real harm, not just a funny story, and the UI is what decides which it is.

Recados were public, unapproved, and a security disaster

The scrap wall accepted HTML, and at points Flash and JavaScript. Consequences, in order: an XSS exploit on 1 January 2005; the MW.Orc banking-credential worm on 19 June 2006; and W32/KutWormer on 19 December 2007, which propagated through scraps and hit 700,000+ users in a day. Separately, an entire Brazilian cottage industry of glitter-text and decorated-image sites existed purely to generate content that got hotlinked into scraps — the feature was extended by third parties because it accepted arbitrary image URLs. Over time correntes (chain letters) became the dominant genre.

“Scrap” entered Brazilian Portuguese as a word. MTV Brasil named a show after it. The vocabulary penetration was total — and the feature was still a worm vector three times over. §07 is why I'm recommending against rebuilding it.

The rest, briefly, because two of these are better than they look

  • The three ratings, assigned by friends and shown as a percentage of them: confiável = smiley, legal = an ice cube (an English pun — cool = cold — which is why it reads as arbitrary in Portuguese), sexy = three hearts. Plus a star for fãs count. All three ratings were removed in the 2009 redesign; only the star survived. The Brazilian clone Orkuti brought them back as an orkutímetro.
  • “Quem visitou meu perfil” (22 April 2006) — visit count plus recent visitors as clickable thumbnails, and you could opt out, at the cost of not seeing others'. That symmetric-consent design is genuinely good and is one of the most-cited nostalgia items. Not one of my three concepts, but worth remembering.
  • Crush list — a mutual-crush notifier, years before Tinder. Also good. Also not now.

04Concept 1 — Comunidades

concept 01

Comunidades: public joinable servers, worn as badges

“Entra em qualquer uma. Aparece no teu perfil. É isso.”

A comunidade is a pqp server with three switches flipped: it's listed in a public directory, anyone can join with one tap and no invite, and your membership is displayed on your profile card. Anyone can create one in about fifteen seconds — a name, an emoji or image, a one-line description, done. There is no feed and no algorithm; discovery is search, a browsable category grid, and the badges on the profiles of people you already know. The names are the content: Eu odeio acordar cedo is a valid community name and a complete joke, and the fact that 6.1 million people once joined a joke is the whole business case. Crucially, nobody is expected to talk in most of them — the 2.35-topics-per-community derivation in §02 says the median community is a badge and always was. So the product is a self-expression surface with a chat room attached, not a chat room with a badge attached. That inversion is what stops you building engagement machinery for a feature whose value is delivered at the moment of the click.

Why it lands with BR users specifically

  • The muscle memory exists. Community-collecting as self-expression is a learned Brazilian internet behaviour with a decade of practice behind it. You are not teaching a mechanic; you are restoring one.
  • Humour-as-identity is the native register. The canonical communities are all jokes — Só mais 5 minutinhos, Imagina se pega no olho?, Anão vestido de Palhaço mata 8. A directory whose top entries are jokes is legible to a Brazilian in one second and to a growth consultant in none, which is a competitive advantage.
  • It's a shape Discord structurally can't copy. Discord servers are private-by-default rooms you're invited to; Discord killed public server-list discovery for most servers and has no concept of “my memberships are my identity.” Copying this would mean inverting their privacy model.
  • It's the answer to the empty-room problem. A brand-new chat app's worst screen is a server with three people in it. A directory means a new user's first session has somewhere to go that already has people.

v1 scope

  • servers.visibility TEXT CHECK (visibility IN ('private','public')) DEFAULT 'private' — one column, one default, nothing else changes. Every existing server is untouched.
  • Public servers get description (140 chars), category, and an existing image_url-style icon field.
  • POST /api/servers/:id/join — joins a public server directly, reusing redeemInvite's ban check and ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING insert without needing an invite row.
  • GET /api/communities — search by name, filter by category, sort by member count or recently created. No ranking model. Two sorts, both explainable.
  • Profile card: up to 6 community badges, user-chosen, plus a count. Requires extending publicUserSchema — a deliberate change to a schema whose comment forbids changes, so make it a considered decision and update the comment.
  • A per-user cap (50, not Orkut's rumoured 1,000) and a per-user creation cap (3/day) via a Postgres-counted limit copying the REPORTS_PER_HOUR pattern — rate-limit.ts is in-process and explicitly single-instance, so anything abuse-facing must be durable.
  • Owner transfer disabled for public communities. One line of code that pre-empts the entire secondary-market problem from §02.
  • Every public server routes reports to the instance queue (listInstanceReports), not just the owner's. Non-negotiable — see §08.

Effort

Server + schema ~2 days · directory API ~1 day · client directory + join + badge picker ~3 days · profile-card work on web and iOS ~1.5 days · moderation wiring ~1.5 days · abuse caps and tests ~1.5 days ≈ 10–11 agent-days, plus a permanent, non-zero, non-delegable daily moderation duty. That last part is not an estimate line item; it's a lifestyle change.

Risks

  • Moderation load is the whole risk and it is enormous. §08 has the numbers; they are worse than you expect.
  • The empty-directory cold start. A directory with 9 communities looks worse than no directory. Seed 40–60 yourself, in Portuguese, funny, before anyone sees it — and be willing to hide the feature behind a flag until it's populated.
  • Name squatting on the obvious jokes, within hours. Needs either first-come-with-a-report-path or no uniqueness constraint at all (Orkut had duplicates everywhere and nobody minded).
  • Badge-farming — join 50 communities to decorate a profile, participate in none. This is fine. It was always fine. Don't build anti-farming machinery for the intended behaviour.
  • Dead communities as the visible default. 90%+ will have zero messages. Design the community page so emptiness looks normal (member list first, a joined-count, a single pinned topic) rather than like a broken chat room.
  • It changes what pqp legally is. §08.

05Concept 2 — Depoimentos

concept 02

Depoimentos: friend-written, owner-approved testimonials

“Escreve um dep pra mim.”

A friend writes a short testimonial about you. It lands in your pending queue and is invisible to everyone — including them-after-sending — until you publish it. Published depoimentos appear on your profile card in the order you accepted them, newest first, and you can unpublish any of them at any time without notice. Only confirmed friends can write; one active depoimento per friend, editable by the author while pending; plain text plus emoji, no HTML, no images, no links (see §07 for why that list is short). The mechanic is not the text — it's the approval. A depoimento on your profile is a thing you chose to display about yourself, written by someone else: the only content on a profile that is simultaneously social proof and self-expression, and the reason Orkut users treated them “como um troféu.” The compose flow ships with a deliberate second option, “mandar como DM”, because §02 proves that if you don't give the private-message use a home, users will make one out of your pending queue and eventually publish it by accident.

Why it lands with BR users specifically

  • It is the single most-named missing feature. Not my inference — Orkut's own founder said so publicly in August 2024, and it's in the headline of nearly every Brazilian retrospective from the 2024 anniversary wave onward.
  • Public friendship declaration is a live Brazilian norm, not a dead one. It just moved to Instagram Stories on birthdays, where it evaporates in 24 hours and can't be collected. A durable, curated version has no current home.
  • The reciprocity loop is the growth mechanic and it's free. Orkut users competed over o top. Writing one creates social obligation to receive one, which pulls the recipient back to the app to approve it, which is a notification with a genuinely warm payload — the opposite of every re-engagement ping in 2026.
  • It fixes a real pqp weakness. A pqp profile is currently five fields. That's a principled privacy stance and a retention hole. Depoimentos fill the profile with content other people wrote and you chose — the cheapest possible profile-depth mechanic, and one that requires collecting no new personal data from anyone.
┌─ Marina Rocha ──────────── marina#4821 ──┐ │ [avatar] amiga desde mar 2026 │ │ │ │ DEPOIMENTOS (4) │ │ ┌────────────────────────────────────┐ │ │ │ "conheci essa mulher jogando valo │ │ │ │ às 3 da manhã e hoje ela é minha │ │ │ │ irmã. te amo, sua louca" │ │ │ │ — bea#0192, jul │ │ │ └────────────────────────────────────┘ │ │ ┌────────────────────────────────────┐ │ │ │ "melhor call da minha vida." │ │ │ │ — rafa#7710, jun │ │ │ └────────────────────────────────────┘ │ │ ver todos (4) → │ │ │ │ [ escrever depoimento ] [ mandar DM ] │ └──────────────────────────────────────────┘ 1 pendente · só você vê · publicar / recusar

v1 scope

  • testimonials(id UUID PK, subject_id UUID→users, author_id UUID→users, body TEXT, status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('pending','published')), created_at, published_at, UNIQUE(subject_id, author_id)) — plus CHECK ((status='published') = (published_at IS NOT NULL)), matching the invariant style friendships already uses.
  • Write gate: areFriendsSql, verbatim. If you're not friends, the endpoint 404s like requireServerMember does — don't leak the existence of a pending item.
  • POST /api/users/:id/testimonials (write) · GET /api/users/:id/testimonials (published only, unless it's you) · GET /api/me/testimonials/pending · POST /api/testimonials/:id/publish · DELETE /api/testimonials/:id (subject unpublishes, or author withdraws while pending).
  • Body: 500 chars, plain text through the existing safeTextSchema control-char refusal. No markdown, no links, no images.
  • Publish is two deliberate taps with a preview showing exactly what will become public. This is the “Não aceita!” mitigation and it is the most important UI decision in the feature.
  • Declining deletes the row. No graveyard, no “declined” state, nothing to mine later — which is also the answer to any LGPD question about it.
  • Blocking someone hides their published depoimento in both directions; hook the existing friendships_end_on_block() trigger.
  • Reportable through the existing createReport path, scoped to the instance queue. A published depoimento is public content the subject chose to publish, which is a genuinely useful thing to be able to say to a moderator.
  • Durable rate limit: 10 written per day per author, Postgres-counted, copying REPORTS_PER_HOUR.

Effort

Schema + service + tests ~1 day · 5 routes ~0.75 day · web UI (profile card section, pending tray, compose sheet with the DM fork, publish preview) ~1.5 days · iOS UserProfileSheet.swift parity ~0.75 day · notification + moderation wiring ~0.5 day ≈ 4–4.5 agent-days. Genuinely small, because it's built almost entirely from parts that exist.

Risks

  • Harassment via the pending queue. A stranger can't write one — friends-only closes most of it — but an ex-friend can. Mitigations: unfriending withdraws any pending item; blocking deletes it; the pending tray shows the author before the text so nobody is ambushed; and the queue is reportable while still private.
  • Accidental publication of something private — the documented Orkut failure. Mitigated by the two-tap preview and by giving DMs a first-class door in the compose flow, not by hoping.
  • Empty-profile asymmetry. People with zero depoimentos will feel it, and popularity-counting is exactly the dynamic Orkut's “auge ou ostracismo” reputation came from. Mitigation: hide the section entirely when empty (don't render “0 depoimentos”), and never show a count anywhere except the subject's own profile.
  • Very low. No new public directory, no new content-at-scale, no bytes stored, no strangers writing to strangers. This is the whole reason it's my pick.

06Concept 3 — Enquetes

concept 03

Enquetes: polls that become threads

“Vota aí.”

A message can be a poll: a question, 2–6 options, one vote per person, live results in the message itself. When a poll closes, the poll message becomes a thread root — one tap turns “28 people voted” into the argument about why they were wrong. That's the pqp-native adaptation: Orkut's enquetes and tópicos lived in separate tabs and mostly stayed empty, whereas pqp already has threads hanging off individual messages (type='thread', thread_root_message_id), so a poll is already the ideal thread root and needs no new container. It's also the smallest concept here by a wide margin, it works identically in a private friends server and in a public comunidade, and it fills a gap so basic that DISCORD_GAPS.md — a 28-item ranked list of everything pqp lacks versus Discord — doesn't even mention it. Discord shipped polls in 2024. pqp has nothing.

Why it lands with BR users specifically

  • Honestly: less BR-specific than the other two. Polls are universal, and I'd rather say so than manufacture a cultural story. Its case is that it's cheap, missing, and useful.
  • The one genuine cultural fit: enquetes were a documented part of the comunidade page, and the Brazilian register of the whole platform was low-effort, high-frequency participation — voting is the smallest possible unit of participation, which is exactly right for a population that joins 50 communities and posts in one.
  • It's the thing that makes a comunidade feel alive without requiring anyone to write a paragraph. If Concept 1 ever ships, this is what stops the median community page from being a member list and a tumbleweed.

v1 scope

  • polls(id UUID PK, message_id UUID UNIQUE→messages ON DELETE CASCADE, question TEXT, closes_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL, created_at) · poll_options(id, poll_id, position, label) · poll_votes(poll_id, option_id, user_id, created_at, PK(poll_id, user_id)) — the PK gives you one-vote-per-person for free, no application logic.
  • Create as part of message send over the existing WS message-create path; vote via a new WS message reusing reactionLimiter's shape.
  • Results broadcast on the existing channel bus — polls are exactly the sort of thing the Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY bus already does well.
  • Question 200 chars, 2–6 options of 80 chars, optional close time (1h / 1d / 1w / never).
  • “Discutir” on a closed poll calls the existing POST /api/messages/:id/threads. Zero new thread code.
  • Anonymous votes in v1 — store user_id for the uniqueness constraint, never expose it. Voter lists are a v2 with its own privacy conversation.

Effort

Schema + service + tests ~1 day · WS create/vote + broadcast ~0.75 day · client poll composer, poll card with live bars, closed state with the discutir affordance ~1.5 days · iOS render-only (vote in v1.1) ~0.5 day ≈ 3–3.75 agent-days.

Risks

  • Genuinely low. No new public surface, no new stored media, no new social dynamic.
  • Poll spam in a busy channel — covered by the existing per-user messageLimiter (capacity 10, refill 2/s); maybe cap open polls per channel.
  • Abusive poll options are a moderation surface, and a small nasty one (“quem é mais feio?”). Deleting the message must cascade the poll — the ON DELETE CASCADE above is doing real work.
  • Opportunity cost is the actual risk. This is the concept most likely to get built because it's easy, and the one least likely to change anything about whether pqp wins.

07The mural (scraps): a deliberate no

The brief offered a scraps-style public wall as an alternative to polls. I looked at it properly and I'd recommend against building it, for three reasons that compound.

  1. pqp already has the capability; the wall only adds the exposure. Scraps existed in 2004 because Orkut had no private messaging — public walls came first and DMs were the retrofit. pqp has DMs, group DMs, dm_privacy controls, and blocks. A public wall adds no new ability to talk to someone. It adds only the ability for anyone to write, unapproved, to a surface the subject doesn't control — which is a pure moderation-surface expansion with no capability gain.
  2. The historical record on this exact feature is three worms. XSS on 1 Jan 2005; MW.Orc stealing banking credentials on 19 Jun 2006; W32/KutWormer hitting 700,000+ users on 19 Dec 2007 — all through the scrap wall, all because it accepted rich content. You'd obviously build a plain-text version. But the reason the wall was culturally beloved was the glitter images, the GIFs, the decorated correntes — an entire third-party Brazilian content economy hotlinked into it. A plain-text scrap wall is not the feature people are nostalgic for, and the feature people are nostalgic for is the one with the worm history.
  3. Depoimentos dominate it on the same axis. Both are “friends write on your profile.” One requires your approval and one doesn't. The approved version is the one Brazilians actually name when asked what they miss, the one that can't be used to harass a stranger, and the one that makes the subject a curator instead of a target. If you're going to build one profile-writing mechanic, build the one with consent designed in.

If you ever want the volume and warmth of a scrap wall, the cheaper move is a birthday prompt — on someone's birthday, offer their friends a one-tap way to write them a depoimento. Same ritual, same warmth, same seasonality, and it inherits the approval gate.

08The moderation and liability bill

This section is about Concept 1 only. Concepts 2 and 3 barely move the needle, and that asymmetry is the recommendation.

What Orkut's public communities actually cost Google

Read this number twice

Roughly 90% of all human-rights-abuse reports that SaferNet Brasil received about the entire internet concerned content published on Orkut. Of those, ~40% were child sexual abuse material. Not 90% of CSAM reports — 90% of all abuse reports about the whole internet, pointed at one platform.

Convictions followed from named communities — Mate Um Negro, Ganhe Um Brinde (two years, TRF-3) and the anti-nordestino communities. The lesson isn't “Google was careless.” It's that a free, public, self-service, search-indexed group primitive with one owner and no default moderation is the exact shape that concentrates abuse — and that Orkut's own aberta/moderada split existed, with the problem living entirely on the aberta side.

The 2026 Brazilian legal position, which is materially worse than 2008's

The finding that should shape the decision

On 26 June 2025, by 8–3, the STF held Art. 19 of the Marco Civil da Internet partially unconstitutional. Platforms can now be civilly liable for user content without a prior court order. And the exemption list — e-mail providers, private video/voice meeting platforms, and instant messaging services — is where pqp plausibly sits today.

Building a public community directory is the decision that moves pqp out of that bucket. That's not a moderation-workload argument, it's a category argument, and it's the single most important thing in this document.

Concretely, what the tese establishes for a platform hosting public content:

  • Presumed liability, with immediate removal expected, for paid/boosted content and content distributed by bot networks — escapable only by proving diligent, timely action.
  • A duty of care (“systemic failure” liability) for massively circulating grave content: anti-democratic acts, terrorism, incitement to suicide or self-harm, racial/religious/sexual/gender hate speech, misogyny, child sexual abuse material and crimes against minors, human trafficking.
  • Crimes against honour stay under Art. 19 — but extrajudicial notification now suffices to trigger removal duties.
  • Affirmative duties on all platforms: mandatory self-regulation with notification and due process, an accessible user complaint channel, a Brazilian legal representative with full authority, and annual transparency reports.
  • Liability is subjective, not objective — negligence or systemic omission must be shown. That's the good news, and it's why the report queue and audit log you already have matter.

Layered on top, and already in force:

ECA Digital — this is live right now

Lei 15.211/2025 (Estatuto Digital da Criança e do Adolescente) was sanctioned 17 Sep 2025 and came into force 17 March 2026. It applies to any digital service accessed by children in Brazil regardless of where the company is, with no small-provider exemption found in the sources I could read. ANPD enforces, with fines, suspension, and prohibition from operating in the country available. ANPD published age-verification guidance in March 2026 and its phase 2, extending to sectors beyond app stores, was scheduled for August 2026 — i.e. now.

Duties include reliable age verification rather than self-declaration, linking under-16 accounts to a guardian, privacy-by-design defaults, a ban on behavioural profiling and targeted advertising to minors, and prompt removal of content involving sexual exploitation, harassment, cyberbullying, hate speech or dangerous challenges. pqp's 18+ gate in services/age-gate.ts — with a real DOB check rather than a “yes I am 18” button — is a genuinely strong starting position here and worth understanding as a compliance asset, not just a feature.

Minimum bar before Concept 1 ships

  1. CONTENT_SCAN_PROVIDER configured and proven. A public directory of joinable rooms where anyone can upload an image, with no scanning, is the Orkut failure mode reproduced with better latency. This is a gate, not a preference — and it's already HANDOVER priority #4.
  2. The instance report queue as the primary destination for anything in a public community, with a same-day SLA you actually keep. listInstanceReports and isInstanceModerator already exist; wire public content to them by default rather than to the community owner.
  3. Server-side takedown that beats the owner. A community owner must not be able to shelter content from you. Check what requireManager-gated report resolution implies here — a hostile owner resolving their own reports is a hole.
  4. A published complaint channel and a removal-request path that a non-user can use. The STF tese asks for it directly, and it's a day of work.
  5. Durable, Postgres-counted abuse limits on creation and joining. rate-limit.ts is in-process and single-instance by design; its own header explains how N replicas multiply every limit.
  6. A rate of report arrival you can personally sustain. One person cannot moderate an open directory indefinitely, and the honest options are: keep the directory small and curated (approval to be listed, free to create), or don't open it. Curated-listing is the version I'd ship.

It is the cheapest of the three, the most culturally specific, the one Orkut's own founder identified as most-missed, and the only one that adds identity depth to pqp's deliberately-bare profile without opening a public surface you'd then have to police. It's built almost entirely from parts that already exist — friendships, areFriendsSql, user-profile-popover.tsx, UserProfileSheet.swift, createReport, the block trigger — and it fails silently and gracefully if nobody uses it: an empty section simply doesn't render. Then, in order

  1. Enquetes (~3.5 days) — while it's cheap, because it's a real gap and because it's the thing that makes comunidades survivable later. Do it as a warm-up, not as a strategy.
  2. Comunidades — the actual bet, and the one worth 10 days. Gate it behind: content scanning live, the instance report queue wired for public content by default, a published complaint channel, a Brazilian legal-representative answer, and a decision that you personally want to be a public-content platform in Brazil in 2026. Ship it curated-listing (anyone creates, you approve what appears in the directory) and let that be a feature — a directory of 60 funny communities that a human vouched for is a better product than 6,000 unvetted ones, and it's the only version one person can run.

What would change the order

  • Content scanning goes live for its own reasons (it's HANDOVER #4 already) → Comunidades' hardest gate disappears and it's worth reconsidering immediately.
  • The “new Orkut” actually ships. As of the most recent sources, orkut.com is an email-capture page and has been announced-but-unshipped since April 2022, with a 2024 interview recycled through 2025 coverage. If it ever launches with comunidades and depoimentos, the window on both concepts narrows sharply — and pqp's advantage becomes that it already has voice, which they'd have to build.
  • Depoimentos gets used more than expected → that's the signal that this audience wants profile-as-identity, which is the thesis Comunidades rests on. Ship the cheap one first partly because it's a test of the expensive one.
The cautionary pattern, stated once

Every Orkut clone rebuilt exactly these primitives, and every one died of distribution rather than design. Orkuti (2014) rebuilt comunidades, depoimentos, recados and even the orkutímetro, crashed on launch from traffic, passed 500,000 Android downloads, then got removed from Google Play over the name and became orkut.one. Orkutando (Aug 2016) followed and is gone. Büyükkökten's own Hello (Jun 2016 – 2022) ported the community primitive as “Personas” and reached ~1 million users before shutting down. And Google's own community archive — 51M communities promised as a permanent time capsule — was deleted on 15 May 2017 with about two weeks' notice and no export path.

The nostalgia converts to signups, not to retention. So the concepts in this doc should be judged on whether they make pqp better at what it already does — group voice and chat for Brazilian friend groups — and not on whether they make it feel like 2009. Depoimentos passes that test (it makes the people in your calls more legible to each other). Comunidades passes it too, at ten times the price and with a liability change attached. A mural fails it.

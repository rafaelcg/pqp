pqp is an open source alternative to Discord: voice, text, watch parties, and screen share with real audio, free. It does not have Discord's third-party bot catalog yet, but it solves the things people actually go looking for in an alternative: calls that do not drop, screen shares that carry sound, and code anyone can read.

This comparison is about what runs today, not a roadmap promise. Where pqp still falls short of Discord, that is written here too.

## Where pqp already does better

**Screen share with audio, no plugin.** On Discord, sharing a screen with audio depends on the operating system and sometimes fails silently. On pqp, sharing a Chrome or Edge tab with the audio checkbox ticked sends the sound along automatically, and the app warns you before you go live if something is wrong (unsupported browser, a muted mic, camera off). See the [screen share with audio guide](/compartilhar-tela-com-audio) for every platform.

**A built-in watch party.** Starting a session to watch a film, a game, or a stream together is a button inside the call, not a third-party extension. A watch party with over 500 people watching at once has already run on pqp, with a mixer to balance the microphone against the film's audio, and a quality you pick yourself (720p or 1080p). Step-by-step guide: [how to watch a film together online](/blog/watch-party-assistir-filme-com-amigos).

**Actually open source.** pqp is licensed under AGPL-3.0. That is not a tagline: the client, server, and mobile app code is public, anybody can read how a feature works, file a bug straight in the repository, or run their own instance. Discord is closed source.

**Discord layout import.** If your community already exists on Discord, you can copy the structure over (categories, channels, roles) by pasting a Guild Template link when creating a community in pqp. It does not copy messages or members, because those are not in a template.

## Where pqp and Discord tie

| Feature | pqp | Discord |
|---|---|---|
| Voice and text in servers | Yes | Yes |
| Roles and per-channel permissions | Yes | Yes |
| DMs and a friends list | Yes | Yes |
| Screen sharing | Yes, with audio | Yes, audio sometimes fails |
| Built-in watch party | Yes | No (needs an extension) |
| Native mobile app | Native Android, iPhone in beta | Yes, mature |
| Desktop app | Yes (Electron) | Yes |
| Open source | Yes (AGPL-3.0) | No |
| Third-party bot catalog | Smaller, younger | Huge, mature |
| Public server directory | Communities with their own address | Millions of servers |

## What Discord still does that pqp does not

An honest way to put it: Discord has a decade's head start on two specific things. The first is its **third-party bot ecosystem**: advanced auto-moderation, music, integrations with every kind of game, built by thousands of developers outside the company. The second is the **sheer size of its public directory**: millions of catalogued, searchable servers.

pqp does not pretend to have caught up on either. What it offers instead is a younger product, without a decade of legacy decisions to carry, with someone who answers fast when a bug shows up.

## When switching actually makes sense

Switching is worth it if:

- Your priority is **watch parties and screen sharing with audio** that work without a workaround.
- You want a genuinely **open source product**, whether on principle or because you want to self-host it.
- Your community is small to medium and does not depend on one specific Discord bot with no equivalent elsewhere.
- You have already dealt with Discord's voice dropping, screen sharing going silent, or you just want to try something else.

It makes less sense today if your community depends on a very specific Discord bot with no equivalent, or if the reach of a giant server directory is the main point.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is pqp free?**
Yes. Voice, text, watch parties and creating a server cost nothing, and the code is open source under AGPL-3.0, you can read it, run it, and self-host it yourself.

**Is pqp just a Discord clone?**
The base is deliberately similar (servers, channels, roles) because that shape solves the problem. What differs is what came after it: a built-in watch party with a mixer and adjustable quality, screen share with audio and no plugin, open source code, and a team that answers fast.

**Can I import my Discord server?**
You can copy the layout: categories, channels and roles from a Discord Guild Template, by pasting the link when creating a community in pqp. It does not copy members, messages or custom emoji, because those are not in the template.

**What does Discord still do that pqp does not?**
A third-party bot ecosystem the size of Discord's, forum channels, and a public directory of millions of servers. pqp has public communities and the Discord layout import itself, but the third-party bot ecosystem is still smaller, simply because it is younger.

## Try it yourself

Switching costs nothing and does not require deleting Discord. Create a community on pqp, invite three or four people to a call, try sharing your screen with audio and a quick watch party. If it works better than what you have today, [create your community](/garanta) and send the link around.
